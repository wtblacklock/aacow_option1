Waco's Allergy Calendar: When to Expect What

Very High
Cedar Fever Season (December – February)

Mountain cedar is Waco's most notorious allergen. Cedar pollen counts regularly reach extreme levels during winter months. If you're miserable in January, cedar is likely the culprit.

High
Spring Tree Pollen (February – May)

Oak, ash, pecan, and elm are major spring triggers for Waco allergy sufferers. Symptoms often overlap with cedar season, making spring especially difficult.

High
Grass Pollen Season (May – July)

Bermuda, Johnson, and Timothy grasses produce heavy pollen loads in Central Texas. If your symptoms peak in late spring and early summer, grass is often the source.

Moderate
Ragweed & Fall Allergens (August – November)

Ragweed, sagebrush, and pigweed are common fall triggers. Waco's warm falls extend this season well into October.

Allergy Shots.
The Closest Thing to a Cure.

Most allergy treatments treat the symptom. Immunotherapy treats the cause. By gradually introducing your immune system to the things that trigger it, we help your body learn to tolerate them — leading to real, lasting relief that doesn't disappear when you stop taking a pill.

Treats the root cause, not just symptoms

Immunotherapy changes how your immune system responds to allergens over time — the only treatment that does this.

Long-term relief — even after stopping shots

Most patients who complete a full course of allergy shots maintain significant improvement for years after finishing treatment.

Safe for children and adults

We administer allergy shots for patients of all ages, from young children to seniors, with careful monitoring at every visit.

Reduces medication dependency

Many immunotherapy patients reduce or eliminate their need for daily allergy medications over the course of treatment.

Common Questions

Questions About Allergy Care in Waco

We get these questions a lot. Here are quick answers — and if yours isn't here, we're happy to talk it through in person or over the phone.

View Full FAQ & Education Center
What are the most common allergy triggers in Waco, Texas?

Mountain cedar (cedar fever) is Waco's most intense seasonal allergen — it peaks December through February and can cause severe symptoms. Spring brings oak, ash, and pecan pollen. Grass pollen peaks in May through July. Year-round triggers include dust mites, pet dander, and mold spores. Allergy testing identifies your specific triggers so we can build a treatment plan around what's actually bothering you.

How long do allergy shots take to work?

Most patients begin noticing real improvement within 6–12 months of starting allergy shots. The full course of immunotherapy typically runs 3–5 years. Patients who complete the full course often maintain significant improvement for years after stopping — and many reduce or eliminate daily allergy medications during treatment.

Do you see children?

Yes. We regularly treat children as young as 4–5 years old for both allergies and asthma. Many of our patients have been coming to us since childhood — and now bring their own kids. Catching allergies early often leads to better long-term outcomes.

How do I know if I need allergy testing?

If you have recurring sneezing, nasal congestion, itchy or watery eyes, asthma flares, chronic sinus pressure, skin reactions, or suspected food allergies — testing can tell us exactly what's triggering you. Many patients have been managing symptoms for years without knowing their specific triggers. Testing takes the guesswork out of treatment.

Do you accept my insurance?

We accept most major insurance plans, including BCBS, Aetna, Cigna, United, and Medicare. Contact our office before your appointment and we'll verify your specific coverage — no surprises at checkout.
